# NIST benchmark map

Use these publications as the qualitative basis for the vendor-review
workflow. Record the publication, revision, and applicable section or category
in every persisted decision packet requirement, finding, and recommendation.

## Primary benchmark

### NIST SP 1326 — Due Diligence Assessment Quick-Start Guide

Official source:
<https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1326/final>

Use this as the default baseline for every vendor review. Its due-diligence
components are:

- Foreign Ownership, Control, or Influence (FOCI)
- provenance
- resilience
- foundational cyber practices
- supply-chain tiers

## Supporting benchmarks

### NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1 — Guide for Conducting Risk Assessments

Official source:
<https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/30/r1/final>

Use its prepare, conduct, and maintain assessment structure. Apply the
threat-source/event, vulnerability or predisposing-condition, likelihood,
impact, and risk reasoning pattern.

### NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0

Official source:
<https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/cswp/29/the-nist-cybersecurity-framework-csf-20/final>

Use the Govern function and the GV.SC category to frame supplier requirements,
due diligence, recorded and prioritized supplier risk, incident coordination,
and lifecycle monitoring.

### NIST SP 800-161 Rev. 1 — Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management

Official source:
<https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/161/r1/upd1/final>

Use this for deeper supplier, product, service, development, integration,
resilience, and supply-chain risk analysis when the vendor's scope warrants it.

## Rule precedence

Use the stricter applicable rule. If an organization policy is explicitly
stricter than the NIST benchmark, apply the organization policy. If policy is
absent, ambiguous, or cannot be shown to be stricter, use the applicable NIST
benchmark and record that fallback.

NIST is a qualitative benchmark, not an automatic approval authority. The
organization's risk appetite and approval authority still control the final
decision.
